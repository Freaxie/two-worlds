# 03 · Shot List

29 shots, about 3.1 s average. 1080 × 1920, 24 fps.

**Source codes:** `ARC` = archival or public-domain image · `STK` = licensed stock footage · `PRX` = practical tabletop shoot with replicas · `GEN` = AI-generated or CGI (prompt IDs refer to **05-ai-prompts.md**) · `MG` = motion graphics.

**Labeling rule:** every *real* object gets a small gold provenance tag (place and date). Every *reconstruction* gets the tag `RECONSTRUCTION` or `ILLUSTRATIVE`. Nothing generated is ever presented as a real artifact.

## HOOK

### S01 · 0:00.0–0:02.0 · `GEN P01`
- **VO:** "Dinosaur bones have been…"
- **Visual:** Ground-level shot. Leather-wrapped feet of a hooded ancient traveler (**"the Wanderer"**, a recurring character) step across wind-scoured, cracked sandstone badlands at last light. Dust lifts with each step. Low sun rakes across from the left, with long shadows.
- **Camera:** Low tracking dolly following the feet, lens about 15 cm off the ground; slow.
- **Transition out:** Continuous move into S02 (camera tilts down and sinks *into* the ground).
- **On-screen text:** Top third from frame 1: **WHY NO ANCIENT RECORDS?** (Cormorant Garamond, off-white; fades out at 0:04.6.)

### S02 · 0:02.0–0:04.7 · `GEN P02`
- **VO:** "…eroding out of the ground for our entire existence."
- **Visual:** CGI geological cross-section. The camera passes through the surface crust. Strata rush upward: tan sandstone, grey mudstone, red siltstone. A huge, fully articulated theropod skeleton lies embedded in rock far below the Wanderer's tiny silhouette at the surface line. Rock texture is photoreal; the bones are dark brown and mineralized.
- **Camera:** Vertical crane-down "through the earth", decelerating to a near-stop on the skeleton.
- **Transition out:** Sub-boom hit, then hard cut.
- **On-screen text:** Small label at the surface line: **HOMO SAPIENS · ~300,000 YRS**. Small label at the skeleton: **DINOSAURS · 66+ MILLION YRS**.

### S03 · 0:04.7–0:08.2 · `PRX`
- **VO:** "So why didn't the ancient world write about them?"
- **Visual:** Macro shot. A reed stylus presses a wedge into wet clay (a replica tablet; **no real or fake text**, just a single wedge). Rack focus to a second, smooth, **blank** clay tablet: an unwritten record.
- **Camera:** Macro push-in, then a slow rack focus.
- **Transition out:** Hard cut on the word "them?"
- **On-screen text:** none (subtitles only).

- **VO:** "Yet no ancient text clearly describes a dinosaur."
- **Visual:** Wide, scale-establishing shot. The Wanderer stands beside a **giant dinosaur femur (about 1.8 m) and a row of vertebrae weathering out of a sandstone cliff**. Only fragments are exposed, which is realistic. Dusk sky, wind, the figure small against the bone. *(This is the "human beside a massive fossil" image.)*
- **Camera:** Slow pull-back and slight crane-up to reveal scale; very slow, mysterious.
- **Transition out:** Hard cut on a percussion hit (the music shifts gear).
- **On-screen text:** Tag **ILLUSTRATIVE**

## INVESTIGATION

### S08 · 0:16.2–0:18.7 · `MG`
- **VO:** "It's not that nobody found giant bones."
- **Visual:** An aged parchment map of the Old World (drawn style, no fake period lettering; modern clean labels only). Four gold pins ignite in sequence: **Mycenae · Qau el-Kebir · Capri · China**.
- **Camera:** Slow push and a slight rotation.
- **Transition out:** Zoom into the Mycenae pin, then cut.
- **On-screen text:** The four place names, small.

### S09 · 0:18.7–0:21.0 · `STK/ARC`
- **VO:** "At Mycenae, over three thousand years ago,"
- **Visual:** The Lion Gate, Mycenae, in low golden light.
- **Camera:** Slow push up the gate.
- **Transition out:** Hard cut.
- **On-screen text:** Tag **MYCENAE, GREECE**

### S10 · 0:21.0–0:23.3 · `ARC`
- **VO:** "someone kept a fossil rhino's ankle bone."
- **Visual:** The actual published photograph of the *Stephanorhinus* astragalus from Mycenae (Meier et al. 2024, open access, CC BY 4.0, credit required), isolated on black, with a subtle 2.5D parallax.
- **Camera:** Slow orbit (parallax) and push.
- **Transition out:** Hard cut.
- **On-screen text:** Tag **FOSSIL RHINO ANKLE BONE · CONTEXT c. 1230–1200 BCE**

### S11 · 0:23.3–0:27.7 · `GEN P04`
- **VO:** "Egyptians wrapped fossil bones in linen, perhaps as relics of the god Set."
- **Visual:** Reconstruction in a torchlit rock-cut tomb. Hands wrap dark, heavy fossil bone fragments in coarse linen strips. In deep background shadow is a carved relief of a Set-animal head (stylized, out of focus, **no legible hieroglyphs**).
- **Camera:** Slow push from hands to the bundle; flickering torch shadows.
- **Transition out:** A whip of torch smoke into the cut.
- **On-screen text:** Tag **QAU EL-KEBIR, EGYPT · RECONSTRUCTION**

### S12 · 0:27.7–0:30.1 · `ARC` + `GEN P05`
- **VO:** "Emperor Augustus collected so-called 'bones of giants.'"
- **Visual:** (a) 0:27.7–0:28.8: the face of the *Augustus of Prima Porta* statue, side-lit. (b) 0:28.8–0:30.1: reconstruction of a cliff-top Roman villa niche on Capri at dusk, with enormous dark bones displayed on a stone shelf by oil lamp and the sea beyond.
- **Camera:** (a) slow push; (b) rack focus from lamp to bones.
- **Transition out:** Hard cut.
- **On-screen text:** Quote tag **"BONES OF GIANTS" · SUETONIUS, AUGUSTUS 72**, then **CAPRI · RECONSTRUCTION**

### S13 · 0:30.1–0:32.8 · `PRX`
- **VO:** "In China, fossil 'dragon bones' became medicine."
- **Visual:** Top-down shot. A wooden apothecary drawer labeled **龍骨** (*longgu*, "dragon bone"). This is the real name of the medicine, written by a real calligrapher or set in a clean font; it is not a fake antique label. Fossil teeth and bone fragments sit on a brass hand-scale, and a pestle grinds a fragment to pale powder.
- **Camera:** Locked-off overhead, a slight slow rotation, then a cut-in to the pestle.
- **Transition out:** Hard cut on a music hit.
- **On-screen text:** Tag **龍骨 · "DRAGON BONE"**

### S14 · 0:32.8–0:35.3 · `STK`
- **VO:** "But most were mammals, like mammoths and rhinos:"
- **Visual:** Two snap cuts of museum specimens on black: (a) a mammoth skull with tusks; (b) a fossil rhinoceros skull. Each gets a thin gold ID line that draws on.
- **Camera:** Quick push-ins (snap zoom, eased).
- **Transition out:** Hard cuts on beats.
- **On-screen text:** **MAMMOTH** / **RHINO** (small labels)

### S15 · 0:35.3–0:38.1 · `MG`
- **VO:** "far younger, and far closer to the surface."
- **Visual:** An animated vertical geological column with a photoreal rock texture. The top layers glow warm gold with small mammal silhouettes. Far below, a dim layer holds a dinosaur silhouette. A depth and age scale runs down the side.
- **Camera:** Starts on the bright top, then dives fast down the column to the deep layer and stops.
- **Transition out:** Match-cut: the deep layer becomes S16's ground.
- **On-screen text:** **MAMMALS: THOUSANDS TO MILLIONS OF YRS** (top) · **DINOSAURS: 66+ MILLION YRS** (bottom)

### S16 · 0:38.1–0:40.1 · `GEN P06`
- **VO:** "Fossilization is rare."
- **Visual:** Overhead time-lapse on a Cretaceous floodplain. A dinosaur carcass is reduced by scavengers and decay to scattered bones that bleach and crumble away. A second carcass nearby is swallowed by flood silt and survives.
- **Camera:** Slow overhead descent (drone-like).
- **Transition out:** Continuous into S17 (the silt layer thickens).
- **On-screen text:** none

### S17 · 0:40.1–0:43.0 · `GEN P07`
- **VO:** "After sixty-six million years, most dinosaur bones lie deep in rock,"
- **Visual:** Cross-section. The buried skeleton is pressed down as layer after layer stacks above it. The camera rises through hundreds of metres of rock, past a marine layer and a coal seam, to the modern surface: grass and a farm field.
- **Camera:** Fast vertical rise, easing at the surface.
- **Transition out:** Hard cut.
- **On-screen text:** **66,000,000 YEARS** (counter ticks up during the rise)

### S18 · 0:43.0–0:45.2 · `STK`
- **VO:** "and what surfaces is usually broken."
- **Visual:** (a) An aerial glide over real badlands (e.g., Dinosaur Provincial Park, Alberta, or the Flaming Cliffs, Gobi). (b) A macro of a weathered, cracked, rock-coloured bone fragment lying among pebbles, nearly indistinguishable from stone.
- **Camera:** Aerial glide, then a macro push.
- **Transition out:** **Cut to black** (the music drops out).
- **On-screen text:** Tag with the real location shown.

## REVEAL

### S19 · 0:45.2–0:47.1 · `PRX`
- **VO:** "The real answer?"
- **Visual:** Near-black frame. A single shaft of dusty gold light falls on a fossil bone fragment on a stone slab. Dust motes drift.
- **Camera:** Locked off. Stillness.
- **Transition out:** Hard cut.
- **On-screen text:** none

### S20 · 0:47.1–0:51.5 · `PRX`
- **VO:** "Finding a fossil and understanding it are two completely different things."
- **Visual:** A vertical split screen of the **same bone replica**. Top half: weathered bare hands turn it by firelight (warm, ancient). Bottom half: nitrile-gloved hands turn it under cool lab light next to calipers and a specimen label. The split line slowly slides.
- **Camera:** Symmetrical slow push on both halves.
- **Transition out:** Hard cut.
- **On-screen text:** Centre: **FINDING ≠ UNDERSTANDING** (the key text moment; held for about 2.5 s)

### S21 · 0:51.5–0:55.0 · `ARC`
- **VO:** "Recognizing a dinosaur takes ideas science only accepted around 1800:"
- **Visual:** Cuvier's engraved plates comparing the jaws of a living elephant and a fossil mammoth, on aged paper.
- **Camera:** Slow pan across the engravings.
- **Transition out:** Paper-grain dissolve.
- **On-screen text:** Tag **G. CUVIER · ELEPHANTS LIVING & FOSSIL · 1796–1800**. On "extinction": the word **EXTINCTION** fades in at centre.

### S22 · 0:55.0–0:58.0 · `ARC` + `MG`
- **VO:** "…extinction, and an unimaginably old Earth."
- **Visual:** Hutton's unconformity engraving (John Clerk of Eldin, 1795) cross-fades into an animated timeline that zooms out. Recorded human history shrinks to a hairline, then to an invisible point, as the line extends past 66 million years.
- **Camera:** A long zoom-out along the timeline.
- **Transition out:** Hard cut into darkness.
- **On-screen text:** **DEEP TIME**, then a scale counter.

### S23 · 0:58.0–1:03.7 · `GEN P08`
- **VO:** "Without them, a giant bone becomes a giant. Or a dragon. Maybe even a myth."
- **Visual:** A firelit stone chamber. The Wanderer raises a huge fossil femur toward the fire. Its shadow on the rough wall slowly morphs into **a towering human-like giant**, then twists into **a winged dragon**, then flattens into **a black-figure vase painting style** (myth becoming art). The vase style is a generic homage; it is not a copy of a specific real vase.
- **Camera:** Slow push toward the wall shadow; the fire flickers. This is the most unsettling beat.
- **Transition out:** The shadow-painting dissolves into the engraving lines of S24 (line-art to line-art).
- **On-screen text:** Tag **ILLUSTRATIVE**

### S24 · 1:03.7–1:07.6 · `ARC`
- **VO:** "Even in 1677, an Oxford scholar examined a broken dinosaur thigh bone,"
- **Visual:** The engraved plate from Robert Plot's *The Natural History of Oxford-shire* (1677) showing the bone; aged paper, raking light.
- **Camera:** Slow push to the bone figure.
- **Transition out:** Continuous into S25.
- **On-screen text:** Tag **ROBERT PLOT · OXFORD · 1677**

### S25 · 1:07.6–1:11.2 · `ARC` + `MG`
- **VO:** "and decided it came from a giant human."
- **Visual:** A ghosted outline of a *human* femur scales up to match Plot's bone (his interpretation). The human outline fades and a gold outline of a ***Megalosaurus*** **femur** locks onto the same shape (today's identification).
- **Camera:** Locked off on the engraving; overlay animation only.
- **Transition out:** Hard cut.
- **On-screen text:** **"GIANT HUMAN"**, which then strikes through to **DINOSAUR (PROBABLY MEGALOSAURUS)**

### S26 · 1:11.2–1:14.8 · `ARC`
- **VO:** "The word 'dinosaur' wasn't coined until 1842."
- **Visual:** A scan of Richard Owen's 1842 *Report on British Fossil Reptiles* (BHL), in macro on the printed word **Dinosauria**.
- **Camera:** Rack focus onto the word.
- **Transition out:** Slow dissolve (12 frames).
- **On-screen text:** Tag **R. OWEN · 1842**

## ENDING

### S27 · 1:14.8–1:21.8 · `GEN P09`

- **Visual:** We return to the Wanderer and the cliff fossil from S07. Now they kneel and lay a palm flat on the giant femur. Wind carries sand across the bone. They look up at the darkening horizon.
- **Camera:** Slow 30° arc around the figure; the slowest move in the film.
- **Transition out:** Hard cut on the breath before "How many".
- **On-screen text:** none

### S28 · 1:21.8–1:26.0 · `ARC/STK`

- **Visual:** (a) 1:21.8–1:23.9: the Rosetta Stone, in raking light. (b) 1:23.9–1:26.0: the Antikythera mechanism, with its corroded gears in macro.
- **Camera:** Slow push on each.
- **Transition out:** Hard cut.
- **On-screen text:** Tags **ROSETTA STONE · FOUND 1799 · READ 1822** / **ANTIKYTHERA MECHANISM · RECOVERED 1901**

### S29 · 1:26.0–1:30.7 · `GEN P10` or `STK`
- **VO:** "And which ones are we holding right now?"
- **Visual:** A modern museum collection store: rows of steel cabinets receding into darkness. A gloved hand slides open a drawer of unlabeled fossil fragments. The camera pushes past the drawer into the dark aisle. In the final 1 s, the texture of a fragment in the drawer **match-dissolves into the cracked badland ground of S01** (a seamless Shorts loop).
- **Camera:** Slow push down the aisle.
- **Transition out:** **Loop** back to S01.
- **On-screen text:** none. (No end card: the loop is the end card.)
